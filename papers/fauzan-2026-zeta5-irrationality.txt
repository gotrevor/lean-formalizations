                            ζ(5) IS IRRATIONAL

                                          Aabir Fauzan
                                      aabir.fauzan@aalto.fi

                                       17 September 2026


                                              Abstract
         We prove that ζ(5) is irrational. The proof constructs integer polynomials Qn of degree
     37n with 0 < Qn (ζ(5)) < exp(−139n2 /5) for all sufficiently large n. These polynomials are
     rationally normalized Hankel determinants. A positive moment representation gives nonvan-
     ishing and, through a logarithmic-energy estimate, bounds their real values. Local estimates
     for the entire rational functional control the coefficients and show that the normalization
     preserves the required decay. A perturbation estimate also gives |ζ(5) − a/b| > b−260 for
     every integer a and all sufficiently large positive integers b.

      Keywords. Riemann zeta function, irrationality, Hankel determinant, Bernoulli numbers,
      logarithmic energy.


1    Introduction
Apéry proved in 1978 that ζ(3) is irrational [1]. For higher odd arguments, much of the
subsequent progress has concerned families of values. Rivoal [10] and Ball and Rivoal [2] proved
that infinitely many odd zeta values are irrational. Zudilin [12, 13] showed that at least one of
ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational. Later work has strengthened the lower bounds for the number
of irrational values [6] and for the dimension of their rational span [5]. These theorems give
information about the family as a whole, but do not determine the irrationality of a specified
odd zeta value beyond ζ(3).
    For ζ(5) itself, Brown and Zudilin [4, Theorem 1] constructed an effective infinite sequence
of rational approximations satisfying
                                          ⃓           ⃓
                                                 p⃓
                                     0 < ⃓⃓ζ(5) − ⃓⃓ < q −0.86 .

Their construction uses cellular integrals and the arithmetic of the associated linear forms. The
exponent 0.86 does not by itself imply irrationality. To obtain irrationality from approximations
of this kind, one needs a decay estimate that remains sufficient after the denominators have
been cleared. The same comparison between real decay and denominator growth also governs
the polynomial construction used below.
    The difficulty of the individual problem is reflected in recent accounts. Lai, Lupu, and Sprang
[8, §1] described the irrationality of ζ(5) as a problem that “seems beyond reach”. Fischler [5,
§1] likewise records the absence of an individual result beyond ζ(3). The present paper proves
the next case.

Theorem 1.1. The number ζ(5) is irrational.
    The proof uses integer polynomials of growing degree, rather than linear forms in 1 and ζ(5).
They are obtained from Hankel determinants whose entries are affine in the polynomial variable.
At ζ(5), a positive moment representation makes the determinants nonzero. The main estimates
show that their values remain small enough to exclude rationality after every coefficient has
been made integral. In this way, nonvanishing and the comparison between real decay and
denominator growth are established for the same sequence of polynomials.
    The construction also gives a bound for rational approximation. This requires a further
estimate ensuring that the determinants remain positive at rational arguments sufficiently close
to ζ(5).

    We begin with an h × h Hankel matrix GK (X), where h = 37K/40, whose entries are
values of a rational functional. Each entry is affine in X. At X = ζ(5), the functional becomes
integration against a positive weight, and GK (ζ(5)) is a positive moment matrix. Its determinant
is consequently positive. A multiple-integral representation expresses that determinant in terms
of a squared Vandermonde factor and the weight. After scaling, the logarithm of the integrand
is estimated by a logarithmic-energy inequality. This gives the required real bound on the K 2
scale.
    The arithmetic estimates must hold for every coefficient of the determinant, not only for its
value at ζ(5). They must also account for the whole rational functional. Polynomial division
produces both polynomial quotients and simple-pole terms, and an estimate based only on
favorable residues would leave the former uncontrolled. We therefore retain both contributions
in the local estimates over Qp [X].
    The main determinant estimates take two forms. For the intermediate primes, a basis with
prescribed vanishing at the pole classes gives a determinant valuation. For the larger primes,

                                               2
another basis isolates the possible negative valuations of the residue terms. The remaining
polynomial contribution is handled by a rank bound. Together with a coarse bound for the
smaller primes, these estimates give a positive rational normalization that turns the determinant
into the integer polynomial QK,M . The cutoff M is kept fixed when the local estimates are
summed. The cost of this normalization is smaller than the real decay. Choosing M = 200 and
K = 40n gives the polynomials Qn above.
    For Corollary 1.2, smallness at ζ(5) alone is not enough, since the polynomial could vanish
at a nearby rational argument. We bound the coefficient matrix of X relative to the positive
matrix GK (ζ(5)). This preserves positivity under the required perturbation and allows the
integer argument to be applied at the rational point. The larger fixed cutoff used in the appendix
provides the additional decay for that argument.

1.2    Organization
Section 2 defines the matrix and proves its degree and positivity. Sections 3 and 4 establish
the local estimates, which are summed in Section 5. The real bound is proved in Section 6,
and the two bounds are combined in Section 7. Appendices A and B contain the rational data
and elementary estimates for the constants. Appendix C proves the approximation bound and
records a coefficient height estimate.

Author’s note on further work
The author is working on extensions to higher odd zeta values and has made several advances
in this direction. Some of the local functional estimates and determinant arguments appear to
extend beyond the present case. The construction for ζ(5) also relies on special features of its
positive moment representation and arithmetic cancellations. Obtaining compatible positivity
and denominator estimates for higher odd values remains a central part of this work.
    These investigations also concern linear independence among specified odd zeta values. The
author is exploring related questions about the transcendence of ζ(5) and selected families
of odd zeta values, as well as algebraic independence involving ζ(5). Developments outside
the argument presented here suggest possible approaches to some of these questions, although
their scope remains unsettled. No such conclusion follows from the irrationality theorem or the
approximation bound in this paper. A general transcendence result for odd zeta values would
require further ideas.
    The author is also actively developing alternative approaches to the irrationality of ζ(5) and
has made substantial progress in this work. These include variants of the present construction
and approaches based on different proof methods. These investigations suggest that the present
construction may not be the only route to the result. The proof given here arose from simplifying
and strengthening earlier approaches developed by the author, and further simplifications or
sharper estimates may be possible. A related aim is to isolate general principles linking local
determinant divisibility, positivity, and real decay.
    These remarks describe the author’s ongoing investigations. The advances mentioned above
are not developed in this paper, and no extension or alternative proof is claimed here.
