## ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — `Literature.StrongSixExponentials` is FALSE as stated

`ExponentialsKnown.not_strongSixExponentials` is a **machine-checked refutation**.  Roy's
theorem requires `x` and `y` linearly independent over the field of *algebraic* numbers; the
frozen `Prop` asks only for `ℚ`-linear independence.  Witness `x = (1, log 2)`,
`y = (1, √2, i)`: `ℚ`-independent, yet all six products `1, √2, i, log 2, √2·log 2, i·log 2`
lie in `𝓛̃` (the first three because `𝓛̃ ⊇ ℚ̄`, the last three because they are `β·log 2` with
`β` algebraic).  Over `ℚ̄` the triple `1, √2, i` is dependent — exactly what the real hypothesis
excludes.  This is also what the degree count predicts: with only `ℚ`-independence,
`λ = 1, s = √2, r = log 2` keeps `λ, λs, λr, λrs` all of degree `≤ 1` in the logarithms, so no
contradiction is available.

## Gotchas worth keeping

* A multi-line `by … using` term inside parentheses does not parse when continuation lines are
  indented under the enclosing `refine`.  Use `show _ ∈ L; rw [h]; exact …`.
* `fin_cases i` leaves indices as `⟨0, ⋯⟩`; `Matrix.cons_val_zero` will not fire.  Either add
  `Fin.zero_eta, Fin.mk_one, Fin.isValue`, or — more robust when a `rw` is needed afterwards —
  restate the hypothesis with an explicit `have h : … := htr` and let defeq do the reduction.
* `LinearIndependent.map'` yields `⇑f ∘ v`; `simp only [Function.comp_def]` before `rw`ing a
  `funext`-proved equality.
* `algebraMap ℚ ℂ q` vs `(q : ℂ)`: `eq_ratCast` as a `simp only` lemma, then
  `linear_combination`.
