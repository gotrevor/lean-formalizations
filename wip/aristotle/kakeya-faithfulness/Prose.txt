Theorem (R. O. Davies, 1971 — the planar case of the Kakeya set conjecture).

Background definitions:

  * Work in the two-dimensional real Euclidean space, i.e. ℝ² with its usual norm.

  * A subset S of ℝ² is called a "Kakeya set" (also "Besicovitch set") if, for every
    direction, S contains a line segment of length 1 pointing in that direction. More
    precisely: for every unit vector v (a vector with ‖v‖ = 1) there exists a point a in
    ℝ² such that the straight line segment from a to a + v is entirely contained in S.
    (No compactness or measurability assumption is placed on S.)

  * "Hausdorff dimension" is the usual Hausdorff dimension of a subset of a metric space.

Statement to formalize:

  Every Kakeya set S in ℝ² has Hausdorff dimension exactly 2.

That is: for every subset S of ℝ² which is a Kakeya set in the sense above, the Hausdorff
dimension of S equals 2.
