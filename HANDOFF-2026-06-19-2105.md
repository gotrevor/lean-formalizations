## The finding: `kakeya_subresolution_content` was UNSOUND

The cited axiom claimed: for a `2ᴶ`-direction net with covered sets `A k ⊆ [0,1]`, covered by pieces
`U n` (`n∈s`) all of `ediam ≤ 2⁻ᴶ` and aggregate covered length `≥ 1/poly(J)`, the Hausdorff content
bound `D⁻¹·cR ≤ ∑' ediam(U n)^d` holds. **False, two independent ways:**

1. **Dropped the `hcR` hypothesis.** The sound brick `caseA_content` links `cR` to `d` via an
   exponential-vs-polynomial inequality (`hcR`). The axiom kept only `0 < cR`, so the conclusion had to
   hold for *arbitrary* `cR > 0` against a *fixed finite* `∑' ediam^d` — impossible.
2. **Even with `hcR`, false for `d>1`.** Base points are arbitrary, so the `2ᴶ` segments can be flung
   apart and covered by *unshared* pieces of size `2⁻ⁱ` (`i≥J`): `≈ 2^{J+i}` pieces, `∑ediam^d ≈
   2^{J+i(1-d)} → 0`. The Córdoba overlap that makes the real theorem work needs the segments forced
   into one bounded Kakeya set — structure the abstraction discarded.

Kernel-checked refutation: `Engine.kakeya_subresolution_content_is_unsound` (derives `False` from the
exact former axiom statement; `#print axioms` = bare trust base, NO `sorryAx`). Config: `J=0`, one
covered direction, one piece covering one unit segment, `cR = vol(disc).toReal + 1`.

## The lesson (worth carrying forward)
A *cited* axiom (faithful restatement of a known theorem) is honest debt; a *self-authored*
"surely-true residual" axiom is unchecked conjecture wearing a proof's clothes — they look identical in
`#print axioms`. Before trusting a self-authored axiom, spend equal effort trying to **refute** it
(instantiate it, hunt a counterexample, try to derive `False`). The danger when lifting a "hard
leftover" into a standalone lemma is silently weakening hypotheses / strengthening the conclusion so the
context that made it true no longer constrains it. Every prior lap cited this one; one real refutation
attempt this lap toppled it in minutes.
