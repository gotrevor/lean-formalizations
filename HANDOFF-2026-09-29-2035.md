## 🧠 Context to carry forward

The lemma says: if the reduced charpoly of `C : Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) ℤ` stays irreducible mod a
prime `p` and `p ∣ tr C^(3^m)` with `m ≥ 1`, then `p ∣ tr C^(3^(m+j))` for some `j ≥ 1`.  The point
of tracking `C` only **up to scalars** is that the projective order divides `p²+p+1`, whose 3-part
is at most 3 — whereas phase 29's `|GL₃(𝔽_p)|` bound carries the useless scalar factor `p−1`, whose
3-part blows up when `p` is 3-adically near 1.  That is exactly where phase 29 was silent.

Two things I learned that are not obvious from the write-up:

- **`p ≠ 3` and `p ∤ det C` are both unnecessary.**  `9` never divides `p²+p+1` for *any* natural
  `p` (the write-up's case split on `p mod 3` is real, but both branches give `v₃ ≤ 1`, and `p = 3`
  gives `v₃ = 0` too), and `AdjoinRoot.root F ≠ 0` follows from `F.natDegree = 3 > 1 = natDegree X`,
  not from the determinant.  The statements are frozen so the hypotheses stay, but a successor
  should know the lemma is stronger than it reads.
- **The route through `AdjoinRoot.lift` is a dead end** — it needs a commutative target, so it can
  never reach a matrix ring.  Abandoned it early.  What works: prove `x^(3^(m+j)) = λ·x^(3^m)` in
  `K = AdjoinRoot F̄`, convert to `F̄ ∣ X^(3^(m+j)) − C λ·X^(3^m)` via `AdjoinRoot.mk_eq_zero`, then
  hit it with `Polynomial.aeval D` (which *is* fine for a noncommutative algebra) and
  `Matrix.aeval_self_charpoly`.

The Mills corollary needed one thing the frozen `ThreeAdic.lean` does not expose: that the companion
matrix's charpoly has `A^(3^m)` as a real root.  Since ThreeAdic is frozen I reproved its
construction locally as the private `exists_companion_root` (a copy of
`exists_companion_of_algebraic_mills` and of the private `exists_vieta_of_cubic_pisot`, plus the new
`companion3_charpoly` and a `linear_combination` off Vieta).  **This is deliberate duplication.**  If
ThreeAdic is ever unfrozen, the right move is to add the root conclusion there and delete the copy.
