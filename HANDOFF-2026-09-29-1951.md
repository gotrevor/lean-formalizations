## 🧠 Context to carry forward

The theorem proved is stronger and cleaner than the Mills application: for **any** integer matrix
`C` with `det C ≠ 0` whose trace sequence `t_k = tr C^(3^k)` is eventually prime and strictly
increasing, `t_k → ±1` in `ℤ₃`. Mills enters only in step 4, as one instantiation.

Three route decisions a successor should not re-derive:

**1. LTE at 3 without the three-case split.** To bound `v₃(tˢ − 1)`, first pass from `s` to `2s`
— free, since `tˢ − 1 ∣ t²ˢ − 1` — because the base then becomes `u = t² ≡ 1 (mod 3)`
*unconditionally*. That collapses the textbook case analysis (`t ≡ 1` / `t ≡ 2` with `s` even /
`s` odd) into one case, plus the cube step `v₃(z³−1) ≤ v₃(z−1)+1`, a `mod 9` `decide` on
`z²+z+1`. I tried nothing else; this was right first time and I'd take it again. mathlib's
`padicValNat.pow_sub_pow` does not cover the `p ≡ −1` branch usefully.

**2. Trace of a matrix power = root power sum, without charpoly machinery.** mathlib has
`Matrix.trace_eq_sum_roots_charpoly` but **nothing about the charpoly of a power**, and
diagonalisation is not worth building. The cheap route: explicit `companion3 a b c`, its
Cayley–Hamilton identity by `fin_cases`+`ring` over 9 entries, then `companion3_trace_pow` by
strong induction on the 3-term recurrence, with `N = 0,1,2` matched against Vieta by
`linear_combination`. Both sequences satisfy the same recurrence and the same initial values.

**3. Where the two halves meet.** Step 2 gives `v₃|GL_n(𝔽_{t_k})| > k`, unbounded in `k`;
`padicValNat_glCard_le` gives `v₃|GL_n(𝔽_t)| ≤ n(2e+n)`, a constant, whenever `3^e` divides
neither `t−1` nor `t+1`. Gauss congruence makes `t_k mod 3^e` eventually constant, so **one**
witness index `k₁ > n(2e+n)` suffices — no need to reason uniformly in `k`.

Working assumption worth stating: the residual case is genuinely residual. If `τ = 1` then
`v₃(p_m − 1) ≥ m+1` and the 3-part survives the `3^m`-th power, which is exactly why Fermat
numbers (`F_k → 1` in `ℤ₂`, the base-2 analogue) escape the method. Don't expect this argument
alone to finish Mills.
