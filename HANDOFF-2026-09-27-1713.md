The one idea worth inheriting: **the prescribed proof plan was wrong about the amount of work
required, and the reason generalizes.**

`DIRECTION.md`'s phase-3 header laid out a four-way case split walking the greedy chain
(`p₁ = 2` vs `p₁ ≥ 3`; then `p₂ = 11` vs `p₂ ≥ 13`; then `p₃ = 1361` vs `p₃ ≥ 1367`; then
`p₄ ≥ 2521008887`), with composite-gap `decide` checks for `8..10`, `1331..1360`, `1362..1366`,
`2521008881..2521008886`. None of that was needed.

`gseq_le_digits` in `RH.lean` (written in phase 2 for the *conditional* result) already performs
exactly that case analysis, uniformly over all `k`, by induction — and crucially it is **already
unconditional**. It takes no `PrimeBetweenCubesFrom` argument. Its induction needs only:
- `lpa_le` — minimality of "least prime above `n`", which rests on Euclid, not on any prime-gap
  conjecture; and
- the *self-generated* step `c³ < ⌊A^(3^(k+2))⌋₊` where `c = ⌊A^(3^(k+1))⌋₊`, which follows from
  the digits of a Mills number being prime (`c ∣ c³`, so `c³` can't itself be the prime digit).

`PrimeBetweenCubesFrom 1` enters `RH.lean` only through `gseq_hi`, i.e. only to know the greedy
chain never stalls — which is only ever needed for the **upper** bound `A < 1.3063778839`.

So `lower_bound_of_isMills` is four lines: instantiate `gseq_le_digits` at `k = 3`, rewrite by
`gseq_three : gseq 3 = 2521008887` and `3^4 = 81` to get `A^81 ≥ 2521008887`, then `by_contra` +
`pow_le_pow_left₀` + `norm_num` on `(1.3063778838 : ℝ)^81 < 2521008887`.

Transferable heuristic: **before decomposing a stated plan, check what an existing lemma actually
*uses*, not what its file assumes.** A lemma sitting in a conditional module can be unconditional;
grep its proof body for the hypothesis name before assuming you need a fresh unconditional version.
